Arkham is both a prison and a psychiatric hospital, and it does neither job well. Its revolving door of villains is almost a joke in the comics, but it also reflects what many stories ask: whether Gotham heals or only locks people up.

## Comics origin

Dennis O'Neil and Irv Novick introduce it in *Batman* #258 (October 1974), its name inspired by H. P. Lovecraft's fictional city. Later it is given a founder in Amadeus Arkham, who turned the family home into a hospital in memory of his mother.

## On screen

- **[Batman Begins](/dc/batman-begins/):** Jonathan Crane, the [Scarecrow](/dc/personajes/espantapajaros/), runs experiments on patients there.
- **[Batman Forever](/dc/batman-forever/) and [Batman & Robin](/dc/batman-y-robin/):** with an exaggerated, colorful look, it is where their villains come from.
- **[Joker](/dc/joker/) and [Joker: Folie à Deux](/dc/joker-folie-a-deux/):** the hospital is far more realistic and cruel. The second film takes place there for the most part, and there Arthur meets Lee.
- **[Batman: Assault on Arkham](/dc/batman-el-asalto-de-arkham/):** the [Suicide Squad](/dc/wiki/escuadron-suicida/) has to sneak inside.
- **[Gotham](/dc/gotham-t2/):** it fills an entire thread of the series, and its inmates cause more than one disaster.
