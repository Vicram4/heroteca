Gotham is a big city on the American East Coast where money, the police and organized crime work hand in hand. In almost every version it is more than a backdrop: its decay explains why someone like [Batman](/dc/personajes/batman/) comes to seem necessary, and why villains like the Joker emerge.

## Comics origin

Batman at first operates in an unnamed city, and Gotham is not identified as his home until *Batman* #4 (December 1940). As Bill Finger told it, he considered "Civic City", "Capital City" and "Coast City" until, leafing through the New York phone book, he spotted a jeweler called Gotham Jewelers. He chose it so that any reader could picture their own city.

## On screen

Each version gives it a look of its own:

- **Tim Burton:** a gothic nightmare built on sets, with an Oscar-winning design by Anton Furst, in [Batman](/dc/batman-1989/).
- **Christopher Nolan:** a modern, recognizable city, shot largely in Chicago for [The Dark Knight](/dc/el-caballero-oscuro/), which makes the danger more believable. Earlier, [Batman Begins](/dc/batman-begins/) shows how the economic crisis has left it at the mercy of the underworld.
- **[Joker](/dc/joker/):** a 1981 city that looks a lot like New York at the time, with strikes, garbage and social rage.
- **[The Batman](/dc/the-batman/):** rainy and corrupt to the core. ||It ends up flooded after the Riddler bombs the seawalls.||
- **[Gotham](/dc/gotham-t1/):** the series portrays it after the Wayne murders, from the precinct where James Gordon works, in a deliberately heightened tone.
