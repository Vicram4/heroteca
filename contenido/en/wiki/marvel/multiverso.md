The multiverse is Marvel's answer to the question of what might have happened. It holds infinite realities, each with its own versions of the characters. That gives plenty of room for imagination, and also the greatest headache for any screenwriter.

## Comics origin

Marvel plays with other worlds very early on, but the idea becomes explicit in the eighties. In 1983, *Daredevils*, Alan Moore's British Captain Britain series, establishes that every reality has its own protector, and the famous numbering follows from there: the main world is Earth-616.

## On screen

### Cinematic Universe

[Doctor Strange](/marvel/doctor-strange/) (2016) introduces the concept, and what starts as a passing note ends up as a whole saga. [Loki](/marvel/loki-t1/) and [What If...?](/marvel/que-pasaria-si/) develop it, and [Spider-Man: No Way Home](/marvel/spider-man-no-way-home/) uses it to bring together characters from other film sagas. [Deadpool & Wolverine](/marvel/deadpool-y-wolverine/) plays it for laughs, and [The Fantastic Four: First Steps](/marvel/los-cuatro-fantasticos-primeros-pasos/) takes place in a world apart, Earth-828.

### Animation

[Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse](/marvel/spider-man-un-nuevo-universo/) (2018) may be its most celebrated use, with several Spider-Men meeting Miles Morales, and [Spider-Man: Across the Spider-Verse](/marvel/spider-man-cruzando-el-multiverso/) takes it further.
