Few events have left such a mark on the Cinematic Universe. Anyone who hasn't seen [Avengers: Infinity War](/marvel/vengadores-infinity-war/) and [Avengers: Endgame](/marvel/vengadores-endgame/) should skip this entry, because it can't be explained without giving away the end of both.

## Comics origin

The idea comes from *The Infinity Gauntlet* (1991). Thanos, with the [Infinity Stones](/marvel/wiki/gemas-del-infinito/) on his hand, snaps his fingers and erases half of all living beings to please Death. The film swaps his motive for a colder one: the universe's lack of resources.

## On screen

||At the end of Avengers: Infinity War, Thanos carries out his plan and half of all life vanishes. Avengers: Endgame undoes the damage but not the time: those who vanished return as they were five years later, which is known as the Blip.||

||Later series and films speak of it as naturally as of a historical event. Peter Parker and his classmates have to repeat the year in Spider-Man: Far From Home, Monica Rambeau comes back in a hospital in WandaVision, unaware her mother has died, and in The Falcon and the Winter Soldier the world doesn't know what to do with millions of refugees.||
