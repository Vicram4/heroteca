The Thunderbolts raise an awkward question: can someone with a terrible past do a hero's job? The answer varies with each lineup, but there are always people with old scores to settle and little trust in anyone else.

## Comics origin

Kurt Busiek and Mark Bagley introduce them in *The Incredible Hulk* #449 (January 1997) as a new team of heroes. The big surprise comes on the last page of *Thunderbolts* #1: they are really the Masters of Evil, led by Baron Zemo, in disguise to win the world's trust.

## On screen

[Thunderbolts*](/marvel/thunderbolts/) (2025) changes the idea but keeps the spirit. Valentina de Fontaine sends a number of operatives to eliminate one another, and they discover that their only options left are to vanish or to cooperate. The group is [Yelena Belova](/marvel/personajes/yelena-belova/), Bucky Barnes, Red Guardian, Ghost, John Walker and Bob. Yelena and Red Guardian come from [Black Widow](/marvel/viuda-negra/), and Walker from [The Falcon and the Winter Soldier](/marvel/the-falcon-and-the-winter-soldier-t1/). ||The asterisk in the title pays off at the end: Valentina presents them to the public as the new Avengers.||
