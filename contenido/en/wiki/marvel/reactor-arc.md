The arc reactor is the image that best defines Iron Man: a blue light in his chest that is both his weakness and his strength. Without it, the shrapnel beside his heart would kill him; with it, he can fly.

## Comics origin

The reactor is a creation of the films, though the idea goes back further. In *Tales of Suspense* #39 (1963), Tony Stark has a heart damaged by shrapnel and wears a chest plate to stay alive. Marvel explained at the time that the armor ran on transistors, and later, as real technology moved on, on integrated circuits.

## On screen

In [Iron Man](/marvel/iron-man-2008/) (2008), Stark builds a small version in a cave, from scrap. It is the reactor his father, Howard Stark, devised with Anton Vanko. [Iron Man 2](/marvel/iron-man-2/) adds two conflicts: the power source is slowly poisoning him, and Vanko's son wants revenge with a version of his own.

In [The Avengers](/marvel/los-vengadores/), [Stark Tower](/marvel/wiki/torre-stark/) already runs on one at large scale. ||Iron Man 3 ends with an operation that removes the shrapnel and lets him toss the reactor into the sea.|| In [Avengers: Infinity War](/marvel/vengadores-infinity-war/) he wears one again, the source of his nanobot armor.
