Where Xavier sees possible coexistence, Magneto sees inevitable war. The Brotherhood grows out of that idea, and what makes it interesting is that its leader has his reasons: he has seen firsthand what happens when a society decides to hunt a minority.

## Comics origin

Stan Lee and Jack Kirby introduce it in *X-Men* #4 (March 1964) with Magneto and a group including Mastermind, Toad and the twins Quicksilver and Scarlet Witch, who later cross over to the heroes' side. Years later, another version carries Mystique's stamp.

## On screen

### Films

[X-Men](/marvel/x-men/) (2000) has Magneto, Mystique, Sabretooth and Toad. In [X-Men: The Last Stand](/marvel/x-men-la-decision-final/), Magneto recruits many more followers through fear of a mutant "cure", and [X-Men: First Class](/marvel/x-men-primera-generacion/) shows the group's birth in the sixties. ||That film ends with the final break between Xavier and Magneto, who spend their lives arguing without ever ceasing to care for each other.||

### Animation

[X-Men: Evolution](/marvel/x-men-evolucion/) turns the Brotherhood into a group of troubled teenagers under Mystique's guardianship.
