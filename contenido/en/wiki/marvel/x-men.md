The X-Men protect people who would rather see them locked up. That sets them apart from other teams: they fight prejudice as well as villains, which is why their stories so often read as a metaphor for any persecuted minority.

## Comics origin

Stan Lee and Jack Kirby introduce them in *The X-Men* #1 (September 1963), with five teenage students: Cyclops, Jean Grey, Beast, Angel and Iceman. The series only takes off in 1975, when *Giant-Size X-Men* brings in an international lineup with Storm and Wolverine, the basis of nearly every later version.

## On screen

### Films

[X-Men](/marvel/x-men/) (2000) opens a long saga that goes back to the sixties with [X-Men: First Class](/marvel/x-men-primera-generacion/) and joins both casts in [X-Men: Days of Future Past](/marvel/x-men-dias-del-futuro-pasado/). [Logan](/marvel/logan/) goes its own way and shows a bleak future. ||Hardly any mutants are left in it, and the team is gone.||

### Animation

[X-Men: The Animated Series](/marvel/x-men-la-serie-animada/) (1992) is the definitive version for many fans, and [X-Men '97](/marvel/x-men-97/) picks it up thirty years later. [X-Men: Evolution](/marvel/x-men-evolucion/) turns them into high school students, while [Wolverine and the X-Men](/marvel/lobezno-y-los-x-men/) puts Wolverine in charge.
