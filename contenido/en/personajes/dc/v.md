V survived a concentration camp in a fascist Britain, and came back with a Guy Fawkes mask, encyclopedic learning and a very long plan: to take revenge on those responsible, one by one, and bring down the regime. He is cultured, theatrical and murderous, and his methods leave open whether he is a hero. We will never know his face.

## Comics origin

Alan Moore and David Lloyd create *V for Vendetta* in the British magazine *Warrior*, from 1982, and DC completes and reprints it in the late 1980s. The work is a direct response to the Britain of its day. Moore later removed his name from the film.

## On screen

### Live action

Hugo Weaving, replacing James Purefoy after shooting had begun, plays him in [V for Vendetta](/dc/v-de-vendetta/) without showing his face, with only his voice and body. The film, written by the Wachowskis with Natalie Portman as Evey, turned the mask into a symbol that later reached street protests.
