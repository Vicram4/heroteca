The Joker is a white-faced criminal with a permanent grin, driven not by money but by proving the world makes no sense. He has no clear origin, and he himself tells conflicting versions. Against [Batman](/dc/personajes/batman/), who refuses to kill, he works as the perfect negative, which accounts for much of his force.

## Comics origin

Bill Finger, Bob Kane and Jerry Robinson introduce him in the first issue of *Batman*, from 1940. His look draws on the silent film *The Man Who Laughs*. He was meant to die in his second appearance but was saved at the last moment, and he has kept coming back.

## On screen

### Live-action films

Cesar Romero plays him as a buffoon in [Batman](/dc/batman/), from 1966, and Jack Nicholson adds gangster and movie star in [Batman](/dc/batman-1989/), from 1989. Heath Ledger won a posthumous Oscar for the anarchist of [The Dark Knight](/dc/el-caballero-oscuro/). Jared Leto plays him in [Suicide Squad](/dc/escuadron-suicida/).

Joaquin Phoenix leads [Joker](/dc/joker/) and [Joker: Folie à Deux](/dc/joker-folie-a-deux/) as Arthur Fleck, a failed, ill comedian, in two films unrelated to Batman. ||In The Batman a future Joker peeks out from a cell in Arkham, played by Barry Keoghan.||

### Series

Cameron Monaghan plays two brothers in [Gotham](/dc/gotham-t5/) who point toward the character without ever bearing his name.

### Animation

Mark Hamill, his most famous voice, returns in [Batman: The Killing Joke](/dc/batman-la-broma-asesina/), which offers a possible origin. Michael Emerson makes a chilling Joker in [Batman: The Dark Knight Returns](/dc/batman-el-regreso-del-caballero-oscuro/), and John DiMaggio voices the one in [Batman: Under the Red Hood](/dc/batman-capucha-roja/).
