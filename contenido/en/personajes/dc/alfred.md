Alfred raises Bruce Wayne after his parents' death and stays beside him when Bruce becomes [Batman](/dc/personajes/batman/). He stitches wounds, tends the Batcave, serves tea and says what nobody else dares to say. Without him, the Dark Knight would be a very lonely man, and many versions give him a past as a soldier or a secret agent.

## Comics origin

He debuts in *Batman* #16, from 1943. At first he is a stout, clumsy butler, a touch of comedy, and he slims down soon after to resemble the actor in that year's film serial.

## On screen

### Live action

Alan Napier appears in [Batman](/dc/batman/), from 1966, and Michael Gough is the only one to last all four Burton and Schumacher films, from [Batman](/dc/batman-1989/) to [Batman & Robin](/dc/batman-y-robin/). Michael Caine brings the most fatherly side to Nolan's trilogy, with [Batman Begins](/dc/batman-begins/) and its sequels.

Jeremy Irons is a wry, handy Alfred in [Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice](/dc/batman-vs-superman-el-amanecer-de-la-justicia/), and Andy Serkis appears in [The Batman](/dc/the-batman/). Douglas Hodge has a brief role in [Joker](/dc/joker/).

### Series

Sean Pertwee plays him throughout [Gotham](/dc/gotham-t1/) as young Bruce's gruff tutor. [Pennyworth](/dc/pennyworth/), with Jack Bannon, tells of his youth in London, before he enters the Waynes' service.

### Animation

He has a voice in many Batman films. David McCallum returns in [Son of Batman](/dc/el-hijo-de-batman/) and [Batman vs. Robin](/dc/batman-contra-robin/), and Anthony Stewart Head takes him to the Victorian era in [Batman: Gotham by Gaslight](/dc/batman-gotham-a-luz-de-gas/).
