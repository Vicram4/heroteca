John Henry Irons designs weapons until he quits, on finding out what his inventions are used for. When [Superman](/dc/personajes/superman/) falls, he decides to honor his memory by building himself a suit of armor and wielding a huge hammer. He has no inborn powers, only brains and a sense of duty, and his name recalls a legend from African American folklore.

## Comics origin

He debuts in *The Adventures of Superman* #500, from 1993, by Louise Simonson and Jon Bogdanove, within the saga that followed the death of Superman.

## On screen

### Live action

Shaquille O'Neal leads [Steel](/dc/steel-un-heroe-de-acero/), from 1997, a very poorly received film. The Steel of [DC's Legends of Tomorrow](/dc/dc-s-legends-of-tomorrow-t2/), with Nick Zano, is another character: Nate Heywood, a historian who turns into a kind of man of steel.

### Animation

Cress Williams voices him in [The Death of Superman](/dc/la-muerte-de-superman/) and [Reign of the Supermen](/dc/la-muerte-de-superman-parte-2-el-reinado-de-los-superhombres/), which adapt his original saga. Khary Payton does so in [Justice League: Gods and Monsters](/dc/la-liga-de-la-justicia-dioses-y-monstruos/).
