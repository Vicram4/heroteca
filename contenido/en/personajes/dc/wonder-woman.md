Diana grows up on a hidden island inhabited only by Amazons and leaves it to confront the war of the world of men. She has divine strength, bracelets that stop bullets and a lasso that compels the truth. She combines the warrior with the diplomat and remains the genre's most recognizable heroine.

## Comics origin

The idea comes from William Moulton Marston, a psychologist who championed a model of heroine who won with more than fists, drawn by H. G. Peter. She debuts in *All Star Comics* #8, from late 1941, and a year later already leads her own title. The film [Professor Marston and the Wonder Women](/dc/el-profesor-marston-y-wonder-women/) tells that story.

## On screen

### Live action

Cathy Lee Crosby plays her, without the classic costume, in the 1974 TV movie [Wonder Woman](/dc/wonder-woman/). Lynda Carter fixes the popular image a year later with [The New Original Wonder Woman](/dc/the-new-original-wonder-woman/).

Gal Gadot debuts in [Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice](/dc/batman-vs-superman-el-amanecer-de-la-justicia/) and leads [Wonder Woman](/dc/wonder-woman-2017/), set in World War I, and [Wonder Woman 1984](/dc/wonder-woman-1984/). She also appears in [Justice League](/dc/liga-de-la-justicia/) and makes short appearances in [Shazam! Fury of the Gods](/dc/shazam-la-furia-de-los-dioses/) and [The Flash](/dc/flash/).

### Animation

Keri Russell voices her in the notable 2009 [Wonder Woman](/dc/wonder-woman-la-mujer-maravilla/). Rosario Dawson repeats in several League films and in [Wonder Woman: Bloodlines](/dc/wonder-woman-linaje/), and Susan Eisenberg, her voice on television in the 2000s, returns in [Justice League: Doom](/dc/la-liga-de-la-justicia-perdicion/).
