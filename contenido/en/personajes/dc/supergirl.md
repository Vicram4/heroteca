Kara Zor-El also escapes Krypton, but she is no baby: she keeps memories of her world and, in many versions, comes to Earth to protect her cousin, who by then is already [Superman](/dc/personajes/superman/). She has the same powers and a different problem: getting out of his shadow. She usually brings more optimism and more vulnerability than he does.

## Comics origin

Otto Binder and Al Plastino introduce her in *Action Comics* #252, from May 1959. There had been an earlier trial run of the name, but this is the one who lasted, with her own stories from the late 1960s.

## On screen

### Films

Helen Slater leads [Supergirl](/dc/supergirl/), from 1984. Sasha Calle offers a far harder, disillusioned version in [The Flash](/dc/flash/), from 2023.

### Series

Laura Vandervoort is Kara in [Smallville](/dc/smallville-t7/) from the seventh season, and Adrianne Palicki goes by that name in an episode of [the third](/dc/smallville-t3/). Melissa Benoist leads [Supergirl](/dc/supergirl-t1/), which opens in National City with a Kara working as an assistant at a media company and hiding who she is. In that show Slater plays her adoptive mother. From there Benoist visits [The Flash](/dc/the-flash-t3/) and [Arrow](/dc/arrow-t5/).

### Animation

Summer Glau voices her in [Superman/Batman: Apocalypse](/dc/superman-batman-apocalipsis/), which adapts her arrival on Earth, and Molly C. Quinn in [Superman: Unbound](/dc/superman-sin-limites/).
