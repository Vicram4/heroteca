This Lucifer, the fallen angel, one day decides enough is enough: he closes hell, hands over the key and leaves. He is elegant, polite and very dangerous, and never tells a lie. He cares above all about one thing, owing nothing to anyone, and least of all to his father.

## Comics origin

DC had already used other versions of the devil, but the one that matters is born in *The Sandman* #4, from 1989, by Neil Gaiman, modeled on the Lucifer of Milton's *Paradise Lost*. The story *Season of Mists* tells of his abandoning hell. Mike Carey writes his own series from 2000 to 2006.

## On screen

### Live action

Peter Stormare steals the film for a moment in [Constantine](/dc/constantine/), a pale, grimy devil delighted to be there. Tom Ellis is the best known, in [Lucifer](/dc/lucifer-t1/), where he runs a Los Angeles club and works with the police, with far more humor than in the comics. He makes an appearance in the [The Flash](/dc/the-flash-t6/) crossover, and Phil Kruse is listed as his double in [season five](/dc/lucifer-t5/). Gwendoline Christie is, in [The Sandman](/dc/sandman/), the version closest to the comic, tall, androgynous and very serene.
