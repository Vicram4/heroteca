Thor is a prince of Asgard with thunder at his command and an ego to match. His father, Odin, punishes him for exactly that, and many of his stories are about learning to deserve the hammer Mjolnir, which only the worthy can lift. Somewhere between myth and comedy, he is one of the Marvel characters whose tone has shifted most.

## Comics origin

He appears in *Journey into Mystery* #83, in 1962, devised by Jack Kirby, Stan Lee and scripter Larry Lieber. In his early run he has a secret identity, the doctor Donald Blake, who transforms by striking a cane. In 1966 the title takes his name. He is also a founder of the [Avengers](/marvel/wiki/vengadores/).

## On screen

### Live action

His first flesh-and-blood version is Eric Allan Kramer's, in the 1988 TV film [The Incredible Hulk Returns](/marvel/el-regreso-del-increible-hulk/).

### Cinematic Universe

Chris Hemsworth has played him since [Thor](/marvel/thor/), in 2011, a tale of downfall and redemption. After [Thor: The Dark World](/marvel/thor-el-mundo-oscuro/), [Thor: Ragnarok](/marvel/thor-ragnarok/) reinvents the character and steers him toward comedy, a path [Thor: Love and Thunder](/marvel/thor-love-and-thunder/) takes even further. ||His failures weigh on him: in Avengers: Endgame he is broken and out of shape, guilty about not having stopped Thanos in time.||

### Animation

He was already in [The Marvel Super Heroes](/marvel/the-marvel-super-heroes/) in 1966. The film [Thor: Tales of Asgard](/marvel/thor-tales-of-asgard/) shows him as a young warrior in training, and he is a regular in the team line-ups of [The Avengers: Earth's Mightiest Heroes](/marvel/los-vengadores-los-heroes-mas-poderosos-del-planeta/) and the LEGO productions.
