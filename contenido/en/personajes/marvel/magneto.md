Erik Lehnsherr survived a concentration camp as a child and drew one conviction from it: humanity will do to mutants what it did to his people. He commands magnetism with colossal force and is often the rival of [Charles Xavier](/marvel/personajes/profesor-x/), whom he truly respects. He is one of those villains it is hard not to agree with at times.

## Comics origin

Stan Lee and Jack Kirby introduce him in *The X-Men* #1, in September 1963, as an adversary with simpler ideas. It was in the seventies and eighties, especially with Chris Claremont, that he was given his past as a Holocaust survivor, and the villain became one of Marvel's most complex characters.

## On screen

### Live action

Ian McKellen brings solemnity to the character in [X-Men](/marvel/x-men/) and [X2](/marvel/x-men-2/), with a famous opening scene at Auschwitz in the first. Michael Fassbender plays his young version in [X-Men: First Class](/marvel/x-men-primera-generacion/), a story of friendship with James McAvoy that breaks forever, and both actors share [X-Men: Days of Future Past](/marvel/x-men-dias-del-futuro-pasado/).

### Animation

He is the great antagonist in [X-Men](/marvel/x-men-la-serie-animada/), the animated series, and in [X-Men: Evolution](/marvel/x-men-evolucion/). [X-Men '97](/marvel/x-men-97/) puts him in an unexpected position: leading the team.
