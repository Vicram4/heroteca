May Parker raises Peter when he is orphaned and is the person who best reminds him where he comes from. She is frail in many versions and surprisingly tough in others, but she always works as the story's conscience. She is also a very practical reason for the hero to feel guilty when he gets home late.

## Comics origin

She appears beside her nephew in *Amazing Fantasy* #15, from August 1962, conceived by Stan Lee and Steve Ditko as a delicate old woman, close to a caricature of the sweet aunt. Her poor health and money worries feed Peter's guilt for years.

## On screen

### Live action

Rosemary Harris is the most classic May in [Spider-Man](/marvel/spider-man-2002/) and its sequels, with a speech about heroes in [Spider-Man 2](/marvel/spider-man-2/) that many people remember. Sally Field plays her in [The Amazing Spider-Man](/marvel/the-amazing-spider-man/), with more bite.

### Cinematic Universe

Marisa Tomei breaks the old-lady image in [Captain America: Civil War](/marvel/capitan-america-civil-war/): a young May who is slow to find out what her nephew does on the side. ||In Spider-Man: No Way Home she dies after an attack by the Green Goblin, and her last words are the famous line about power and responsibility.||

### Animation

She has been a regular supporting character since the 1967 series [Spider-Man](/marvel/spider-man-1967/). The most different version is Lily Tomlin's, in [Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse](/marvel/spider-man-un-nuevo-universo/). ||That May keeps an arsenal in the basement and acts as an accomplice to every Spider-Man who reaches her house.||
