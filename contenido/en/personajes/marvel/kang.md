Kang comes from a distant future with impossible technology and one simple desire: to rule everything. What makes him special is that there is never just one Kang, because his time travel keeps creating versions of himself who even fight each other. He is the ideal villain for stories about fate and its paradoxes.

## Comics origin

Stan Lee and Jack Kirby introduce him in *Fantastic Four* #19, from October 1963, as the pharaoh Rama-Tut, and he returns as Kang in *The Avengers* #8, from September 1964. His real name is Nathaniel Richards, a distant descendant of the family of [Reed Richards](/marvel/personajes/mr-fantastico/).

## On screen

### Cinematic Universe

Jonathan Majors plays him in two very different versions. ||At the end of the first season of Loki he is He Who Remains, a weary, playful man whom Sylvie kills, unleashing the multiverse.|| [Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania](/marvel/ant-man-y-la-avispa-quantumania/) presents an exiled, menacing Kang, and [Loki](/marvel/loki-t2/) adds one more variant in its second season. Marvel later turned its multiverse saga toward [Doctor Doom](/marvel/personajes/doctor-doom/).

### Animation

Jonathan Adams voices him in [The Avengers: Earth's Mightiest Heroes](/marvel/los-vengadores-los-heroes-mas-poderosos-del-planeta/), where he is one of the major villains, and he also appears in [The Avengers: United They Stand](/marvel/the-avengers-united-they-stand/).
