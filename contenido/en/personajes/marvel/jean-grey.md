Jean Grey can read minds and move objects with her thoughts, and for a long time she was the only woman among the original X-Men. Her story has a before and an after: the arrival of Phoenix, a cosmic force that multiplies her power beyond control. Nearly all her versions play with the question of what remains of her when that force takes over.

## Comics origin

Stan Lee and Jack Kirby create her for *The X-Men* #1, from September 1963, under the code name Marvel Girl. Chris Claremont and John Byrne make her a star with *The Dark Phoenix Saga*, from 1980, one of Marvel's most influential stories, whose tragic ending has been adapted again and again.

## On screen

### Live action

Famke Janssen plays her in [X-Men](/marvel/x-men/) and [X2](/marvel/x-men-2/). ||She sacrifices herself at the end of that second film, and in X-Men: The Last Stand she returns as Phoenix, destructive and out of control.|| Sophie Turner is the young version in [X-Men: Apocalypse](/marvel/x-men-apocalipsis/) and [Dark Phoenix](/marvel/x-men-fenix-oscura/), a second attempt at adapting the famous saga. Sadie Sink plays her in [Spider-Man: Brand New Day](/marvel/spider-man-brand-new-day/), now within the Cinematic Universe.

### Animation

[X-Men](/marvel/x-men-la-serie-animada/), the animated series, adapts the Phoenix saga quite faithfully. Jennifer Hale voices her in [Wolverine and the X-Men](/marvel/lobezno-y-los-x-men/) and in [X-Men '97](/marvel/x-men-97/), where her story has one of its most talked-about twists.
